Modeling the Information Accountability in Software Systems

Open Bachelor's thesis · Modeling, Accountability, Data protection

Motivation

(Personal) Data is now collected, shared, and combined at a scale that individuals can no longer follow. Privacy protection has traditionally focused on controlling access: keeping data secret and deciding in advance who may see it. This approach alone is no longer enough, as claimed by [2]. Once information has been disclosed, it can be copied, aggregated, and reused in ways that access control cannot prevent. As a complementary approach, Weitzner et al. [2] propose information accountability, i.e., the use of information should be transparent so that it is possible to determine afterwards whether a particular use was appropriate under the applicable rules and to hold those responsible for misuse accountable. Regulation such as the GDPR [1] points in the same direction. It requires data controllers to be able to demonstrate compliance (Art. 5(2)) and grants individuals a right of access to information about how their data is processed (Art. 15).

In practice, organizations already log access to personal data, but those logs are built for admins and auditors, not for the people to whom the data pertains. As far as end-users are concerned, they cannot easily see who accessed their data, why, under which legal basis, or what came of it.

References

  • [1] European Parliament and Council. Regulation (EU) 2016/679, 2016. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj.
  • [2] Daniel J. Weitzner, Harold Abelson, Tim Berners-Lee, Joan Feigenbaum, James Hendler, and Gerald Jay Sussman. Information accountability. Commun. ACM, 51(6):82–87, June 2008.
